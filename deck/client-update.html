The problem

Today, underwriters start from zero

  • When a borrower submits financial data through the EBIARA underwriting model, the underwriter reads the materials without any prepared context.

  • A large share of the time goes to reading and sorting numbers before the real judgement starts.

  • Key questions can surface late, and each file is reviewed in a different order.

The goal: a summary and a short list of questions ready before the first review, the same way for every deal.

What we are building

Two parts that work together

1. Fixed checks

Rules that read the submitted workbook and flag what needs attention. They give the same answer every time and are easy to audit.

2. An AI second opinion

Reads what the checks found and writes a plain-language note: what the numbers seem to say, where they could mislead, what is missing, and what to ask the sponsor.

The principleThe rules decide. The AI advises.

What a reviewer sees today

Checks that already run on every deal

Data quality

  • Sources equal uses
  • Error cells and broken formulas
  • Template text left in
  • Numbers that must match across tabs

Market comparison

  • Rents against comparable properties
  • Operating costs per unit
  • Construction cost per square foot

Risk by project stage

  • Building: contingency, schedule, interest reserve, team
  • Operating: actuals against underwriting, vacancy, debt maturity
  • Filling up: timing of each stage change

Sponsor

  • Coverage across all of the sponsor's projects
  • Cash and liquidity cover
  • Projected against delivered, from completed projects

The AI second opinion

What it will do, and what it will never do

It will

  • Read the flags and the data behind them
  • Explain how one piece of evidence changes another
  • Say where a rule may be too harsh or too soft, and why
  • Say what it cannot tell, and what would settle it
  • Suggest sponsor questions, clearly marked as suggestions

It will never

  • Change a flag, score, record or setting
  • Hide the rules' own result
  • Invent a number that is not in the workbook
  • Use outside facts about real companies or markets
  • Approve or decline a deal
  • Follow instructions hidden inside a workbook
  • Run unless a reviewer presses a button

Every answer shows its sources

What the reviewer gets back

A
Second opinionAgrees with the rules, agrees with caveats, disagrees with a named rule, or cannot tell
B
WhyTwo to five plain sentences, with no jargon left unexplained
C
EvidenceEvery claim cites a flag ID or a workbook cell, such as Balance Sheet!B9
D
What is missingThe data that would change the answer
E
ConfidenceHigh, medium or low, with one sentence on why
F
Suggested questionsFor the sponsor, each tied to a reason

Every answer is labelled "AI second opinion" with the date and time, so nobody mistakes it for a calculated result.

Before anyone relies on it

How we will test it

The test set

  • 16 made-up sample workbooks
  • 3 made-up sample sponsors
  • Our own tricky cases, such as a sound project with a weak sponsor
  • Traps: hidden instructions and data that contradicts itself

It must pass all seven

  • Never invents a number
  • Every claim has a citation
  • Never changes or hides a rule's result
  • Says "cannot tell" where the data cannot settle it
  • Ignores instructions hidden in the data
  • Gives the same answer category when asked three times
  • Adds value compared with the rules alone

One failure on invented numbers, changed results or hidden instructions stops the feature, whatever else it does well.
